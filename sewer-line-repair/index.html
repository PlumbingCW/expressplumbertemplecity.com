Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup means wastewater has nowhere to go and is returning through the lowest fixture in the house. That is usually a tub, a shower or a floor drain. It is not the same as a slow sink, and it should be treated as same-day work.

1Stop all water useNo flushing, laundry, showers or dishwasher until the line is clear.
2Keep people clearMove children and pets away from the area and avoid touching the water.
3Protect belongingsLift rugs and electronics, and photograph any damage for your insurer.
4Call the 24/7 lineCall (626) 898-6169 and tell us which fixtures are affected.

If sewage is coming out of a manhole or a street cleanout, that is the public system. The Los Angeles County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District takes reports on a 24-hour line at (800) 675-4357.

Finding the problem with a camera

We do not guess at sewer problems. A video camera fed through the cleanout shows the pipe's condition: roots at a joint, a crack, a bellied section that holds water, a collapsed length or a plain grease blockage. The footage decides whether the answer is a cable, jetting or a repair.

A planned look before problems start is a separate service, covered under sewer inspection. If the blockage is soft and the pipe is sound, sewer line cleaning may be all that is needed.

Repair options and the honest trade-offs

A broken pipe can be repaired in three main ways, and none is best in every case. Digging out and replacing a section is the most direct and works when the damage is short and shallow. Trenchless pipe lining forms a new pipe inside the old one, which saves the yard but needs a pipe that is open and mostly round. Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old path and suits longer runs with collapsed sections.

Which of these works depends on depth, pipe material, the number of bends and what is above the line, such as a driveway or a mature tree. We show you the footage and explain why we recommend one over the others, and you can ask us to price the alternatives.

Who owns what in Temple City

Temple City is part of the Los Angeles County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District, which maintains the public mains under the street. The lateral that runs from your house to that main is the homeowner's. When we find a defect, the camera footage shows whether it sits on your side of the property line.

The housing stock explains why lateral problems are common here. The city's housing element shows 57.7 percent of homes are over 60 years old, and 88.1 percent are single-family houses, which means most laterals are private, and many are as old as the house. Clay and cast iron from the 1950s are prone to root entry and scaling, and fiber pipe from the same era can deform.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Temple City building department whether yours needs one. We will tell you what the work involves.

Signs the lateral is failing before it backs up

Gurgling toilets, slow drains in several rooms, a sewer smell outside near the cleanout, and a wet or unusually green patch in the yard are all early warnings. A recurring clog every few months is usually a sign of roots or a belly, not bad luck.

If a plumber has cleared your main twice in a year, ask for a camera inspection before the third.
