Glacier Bay Kitchen Faucet Repair: A Temple City Guide

Most kitchen faucet problems come down to a worn cartridge, a clogged aerator or a loose connection, and many can be fixed with a few tools. This guide covers common Glacier Bay kitchen faucet repairs and when it is time to call a plumber.

By the Temple City Express Plumbing team · November 13, 2026

24/7 Emergency Line Open

Find your model before buying parts

Glacier Bay is a house brand sold at home improvement stores, and it covers many styles of faucet. Parts are not interchangeable between models. Before you buy a cartridge or a repair kit, find the model number. It is often printed on the box, in the manual or stamped on the faucet body or a tag on the supply line.

If you cannot find it, take the old cartridge to the store, or take clear photos of the faucet, the base and the underside. Matching parts by eye is a common way to end up with the wrong one.

Common kitchen faucet problems and causes

Kitchen faucet symptoms and likely repairs
SymptomLikely causeTypical repair
Drip from the spoutWorn cartridge or ceramic discReplace the cartridge
Handle is loose or wobblesLoose set screw or handle adapterTighten or replace the adapter
Low flow or uneven sprayClogged aeratorUnscrew and clean the aerator
Leak under the sinkSupply line or base sealReplace lines or O-rings
Pull-down hose will not retractWeight or hose tangleReposition the weight or replace the hose

Most single-handle kitchen faucets, including many Glacier Bay models, use a replaceable cartridge or ceramic disc. Two-handle models use a stem or cartridge for each side.

Under CALGreen standards, California caps faucet flow, so choose a replacement aerator with the same rated flow as the original.

Fixing a drip, step by step

  1. 01

    Shut off water

    Close the two angle stops under the sink, and open the faucet to release pressure.

  2. 02

    Remove the handle

    Pop off the cap, loosen the set screw and lift the handle.

  3. 03

    Pull the cartridge

    Remove the retaining clip or nut, note the orientation and lift out the cartridge.

  4. 04

    Replace and test

    Fit the new cartridge in the same position, reassemble and open the stops slowly.

Put a towel in the sink to catch dropped screws. If the cartridge will not come out, do not force it. Mineral deposits can lock it in, and a puller made for the model may be needed.

Cleaning the aerator

The aerator is the small screen at the tip of the spout. When it clogs, flow drops and the stream sprays sideways. Unscrew it by hand or with a cloth and pliers, rinse out grit and soak it in vinegar for an hour to dissolve scale. Rinse and screw it back on.

If a faucet loses pressure at the spout but not at other taps, the aerator is the first thing to try. If every faucet in the house has lost pressure, the cause is somewhere else, and our faucet and valve repair page explains what we check.

When the repair is not worth it

A faucet with a corroded body, a cracked spout or repeated leaks is better replaced. Parts for a low-cost faucet can cost a good fraction of a new one. If you are replacing it, the supply lines and shutoff valves under the sink are worth replacing at the same time.

If a shutoff will not close or leaks when you turn it, stop. Close the main valve and call. A stuck angle stop can break if forced. For a larger job, see our plumbing installation page.

Hard water and faucets in Temple City

Golden State Water's South Arcadia system averaged 160 mg/L in 2025, and its South San Gabriel system averaged about 240 mg/L, which is about 14 grains per gallon. Water of that kind leaves white scale that clogs aerators and stiffens cartridges. A kitchen faucet in Temple City can need aerator cleaning every few months.

Houses built in the 1950s make up 30.6 percent of Temple City's housing, and many still have original shutoff valves under the sink. Those valves can seize, so it is smart to test them once a year. Turn each one closed and open again, and replace any that stick.

The same advice applies in nearby cities such as El Monte and San Marino, where water supply and housing age differ, and the local pages explain how.

Pull-down sprayer and hose problems

Many kitchen faucets have a pull-down or pull-out sprayer on a flexible hose. When the sprayer does not return to its dock, the weight on the hose has often slipped, or the hose has kinked under the sink. Check that the weight sits on the hose and can slide freely, and that nothing in the cabinet blocks the hose.

If water sprays from the hose or the sprayer head, check the quick-connect fittings at the hose ends. These fittings use a plastic clip or O-ring that can wear. Replacing the hose is a simple repair if you can reach the connections. If the leak is at the base of the faucet, the base gasket or the deck seal is the likely cause.

Replacing supply lines and shutoffs while you are there

Under the sink are two flexible supply lines and two angle stops. They get less attention than the faucet, and when they fail they cause more damage. Braided supply lines wear, and the connections can corrode or seep.

If your faucet is a few years old and the lines look worn, replace them while you have the cabinet open. Turn each stop off and on to test it first. If a stop will not close, or drips at the stem, our faucet and valve repair service replaces it. Do not force a stuck valve.

Call a plumber if

Call if the faucet is leaking under the sink and you cannot shut it off, if the base is corroded, if the cartridge will not come out or if water damage shows in the cabinet. Call (626) 898-6169 and describe the faucet.
