When a failed tank is an emergency replacement

A tank that leaks from the seam or the bottom has finished its working life. Water heaters hold 40 to 50 gallons in most houses, and a failed liner can empty that onto a garage floor or through a hall closet in an hour or two.

Our job on an emergency call is to stop the damage first, then get hot water back. That means closing the supply, draining what is left, and removing the old unit. If the house has had a leak, we recommend you photograph it before anything is moved and keep the old tank's data plate for your insurer.

If a replacement can be done the same day, we do it. If parts or a sized unit are not on the truck, we tell you plainly on the phone and book the earliest slot.

How a same-day replacement goes

  1. 01

    Shutoff and drain

    Water and fuel are isolated and the tank is drained to a safe point.

  2. 02

    Remove and prepare

    The old heater comes out and the area is dried and cleared.

  3. 03

    Set and connect

    The new tank is set, leveled and connected with new flexible lines or rigid piping as the layout calls for.

  4. 04

    Safety items and test

    Relief valve discharge, venting, bracing and fuel connections are checked, then the heater is filled, lit and tested.

A replacement is normally permit work under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the new tank must be braced under California Health and Safety Code Section 19211. Ask the Temple City building department what your job needs.

What to do before we arrive: shut off the cold supply to the failed tank, switch off the gas control or breaker and keep people away from any hot water draining from the relief line.

Sizing and fuel: tank, tankless or hybrid

Most houses in Temple City are single-family homes, so a 40 or 50 gallon tank is the usual match, with larger tanks for bigger households. We match the replacement to the house, not to the old unit's label, because family size and fixtures change over the years.

A tank is not the only choice. On the tankless water heater page we explain what changes on the gas line and venting, and water heater installation covers a planned swap when nothing is leaking yet.

Code items that come with every replacement

Temple City's municipal code adopts the 2025 California Plumbing Code, and a few items apply to nearly every water heater swap. The relief valve needs a properly routed discharge pipe, venting must match the fuel, and gas connections need to be tested for leaks before the heater is lit.

California Health and Safety Code 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquakes. We strap every heater we set. A strapped tank is far less likely to tip, rupture a gas line and start a fire in an earthquake.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Temple City building department whether yours needs one. We can tell you what the work involves so you can ask the right question.

Hard water and how long a new heater lasts

Golden State Water's South San Gabriel system reported an average 240 mg/L hardness in 2025, about 14 grains per gallon, and its South Arcadia system averaged 160 mg/L. Harder water leaves more scale on the bottom of the tank.

A yearly flush and an anode rod check slow that down. If you want to cut the scale at the source, our water softener and filtration page explains how, and it also notes that a softener changes your water, so test first.
