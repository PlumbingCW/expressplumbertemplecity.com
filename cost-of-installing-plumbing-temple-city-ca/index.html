The Cost of Installing Plumbing for Temple City Homeowners

The cost of installing plumbing depends on the layout, the access and the fixtures far more than on the plumber's hourly rate. This guide lists what drives the cost in a Temple City home and how to compare quotes.

By the Temple City Express Plumbing team · November 13, 2026

24/7 Emergency Line Open

Why there is no single number

People often ask what it costs to install plumbing, and the honest answer is that it depends on the project. A new toilet in an existing spot is a short job. A new bathroom in an addition requires supply lines, drain lines, vents, fixtures and often a permit. The price range between those two is wide.

We do not quote prices in a guide because the cost depends on your house. What we can do is explain what moves the price, so that when you compare quotes you can see why they differ.

Seven things that move the price

Distance from existing linesNew fixtures close to existing supply and drain lines cost less.
Foundation typeConcrete slabs need cutting, raised floors give access.
Wall and ceiling accessFinished walls need opening and patching.
Fixture selectionThe fixtures you choose often cost more than the labor.
Pipe materialCopper and PEX are priced and installed differently.
Permits and inspectionsSome work needs the city's sign-off.
Existing conditionsOld pipe may need replacing before new work is connected.

Permits and inspections under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), are part of what a fair quote covers, so ask whether they are included.

Rough-in and finish are separate stages

Plumbing work usually comes in two stages. Rough-in is the pipe work that goes in before walls close, and finish is the fixtures and connections after. A quote may bundle both or separate them, so ask which is included.

How to compare quotes

A lower quote is not always cheaper. Ask what is included, who buys the fixtures, whether permits and inspections are in the price, how much wall repair is covered and what happens if the plumber finds old pipe that must be replaced. Ask for an itemized quote and compare line for line.

Be wary of a quote that does not mention permits for work that clearly needs one. A permit protects you when you sell the house and when your insurer asks what was done.

Old pipe in the walls

If walls are open, it is the cheapest time to replace old supply lines. Galvanized steel and aging copper can be swapped while the access is already there. Doing it later means opening the walls again. If your house has had several leaks, our repiping page explains how to decide.

Permits and planning in Temple City

Temple City's municipal code adopts the 2025 California Plumbing Code, so new work is built to that edition. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Temple City building department whether yours needs a permit and which inspections follow.

Single-family homes make up 88.1 percent of Temple City's housing units, so most installation projects are remodels and additions on existing lots. The city's housing element shows 57.7 percent of homes are more than 60 years old, so many projects uncover older pipe that has to be dealt with.

If you live in a nearby city, such as San Gabriel or El Monte, the permit office is that city's. Housing stock there is also older, which affects the work in the same way.

A scope walkthrough: adding a second bathroom

Take a second bathroom as an example. The job starts with a plan for where the fixtures go. The plumber then runs a hot and cold supply from the nearest existing lines, a drain that slopes to an existing stack or a new connection, and a vent that reaches open air. In a house on a slab, those drains may mean cutting concrete. In a house with a crawlspace, the lines can run underneath.

After rough-in, the walls are closed, and the fixtures are set. The work is tested, inspected where the city requires it and finished. Each stage adds labor and materials. A bathroom on the other side of a wall from existing plumbing costs far less than one at the opposite end of the house, which is why layout matters more than the fixtures.

How to keep the cost reasonable

  • ✓Keep new fixtures close to existing lines when the layout allows.
  • ✓Decide on fixtures before rough-in, so the openings match.
  • ✓Bundle jobs. A plumber who is already opening a wall can replace old valves at a low added cost.
  • ✓Ask for an itemized quote and keep the scope in writing.
  • ✓Avoid changes after the walls close, which are expensive.

Cutting corners on materials or permits tends to cost more later. If a quote is far below the rest, find out what is missing from it.

What to have ready when you ask for a quote

A plumber can give a more useful answer when you bring a few details. Have the approximate age of the house, the foundation type if you know it and a rough sketch of where the fixtures will go. Photos of the existing plumbing, the water heater, the main shutoff and any cleanouts help too.

Say whether the walls are open or will be, whether other trades are involved and when you hope to start. If you are buying fixtures yourself, share the model numbers, since rough-in dimensions depend on them. A quote built on real information is far closer to the final bill than one built on guesses.

Questions to ask before you hire

Check that the plumber holds an active license on the Contractors State License Board's License Check page. Ask about the permit, the warranty on labor and parts, and who cleans up. Ask for a start and finish plan. If the answers are vague, keep looking.
