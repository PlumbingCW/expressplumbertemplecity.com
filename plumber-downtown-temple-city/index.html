What Downtown Temple City is, and what it is not

Downtown Temple City is a commercial and mixed-use district, not a residential subdivision. The city's zoning code describes its downtown zone as a pedestrian-oriented downtown along Las Tunas Drive, and a separate Las Tunas Commercial zone supports mixed commercial uses around it. Our own office is on the same street, at 9934 E Las Tunas Dr.

That shapes the calls. A leak in a shop, a slow floor drain in a restaurant kitchen or a water heater serving a small office is a different job from a bathroom fixture in a house. The questions are who owns the pipe, where the shutoff is and whether the business can stay open while the work is done.

Plumbing issues common to storefronts and mixed-use buildings

This section is general information about this kind of building, not a statement about any particular address on Las Tunas Drive.

Floor drains and kitchen linesRestaurants and food shops send grease and food solids down the same lines all day. A drain that slows on a Friday is often a buildup problem, and drain cleaning clears it. Lines that clog again soon may need hydro jetting.
Shared lines in mixed-use buildingsWhen apartments sit above shops, drains and risers can serve several tenants. A backup in one unit may start in a line shared with others, and a camera shows where.
Hidden leaks behind finished wallsA water bill that climbs with no visible cause, or a stain on a shared ceiling, points to leak detection before anyone opens a wall.
Old or buried sewer lateralsSlow drains across several fixtures can mean a lateral problem. A sewer inspection with a camera shows the line before a repair is quoted.

For ongoing needs such as water heaters, restroom fixtures and tenant improvements, our commercial plumbing page lists what we handle for businesses.

Tenant, landlord and who calls the plumber

In a leased space, the lease usually decides who pays for a plumbing repair, and that is a matter between you and your landlord. We cannot tell you what your lease says. What we can do is explain clearly what failed and what the fix involves, which helps whoever is responsible make a decision.

If you are a tenant, tell the property manager before you book work unless water is actively escaping. If water is escaping, close the nearest shutoff and then call. In a building with a shared supply, the shutoff for your space may be in a common area, so ask where it is before an emergency rather than during one.
