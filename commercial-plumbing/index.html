After-hours plumbing emergencies for businesses

Commercial plumbing problems cost money by the hour. A restaurant cannot open with a sewer backup. A shop cannot serve customers with a flooded restroom. We answer the emergency line at any hour, including nights, weekends and holidays, and same-day visits are available when we have an opening.

While you wait, locate your shutoffs. Most commercial buildings have a main valve, and many have isolation valves by restroom groups and kitchen areas. Closing the one nearest the problem limits the damage while you keep the rest of the building open.

We work on small and mid-size commercial properties. If a job needs a large construction crew or a specialist trade, we say so up front instead of promising it.

Commercial work falls under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Restaurants and other food businesses face extra rules, such as grease interceptor requirements set by local sewer and health authorities.

Grease, drains and restaurants

Kitchen drains in a restaurant take far more grease than a home's. A grease trap or interceptor catches some of it, but fat still builds on the pipe wall downstream. Regular cabling clears the plug and hydro jetting cleans the wall, which keeps the line from backing up on a Saturday night.

Grease trap and interceptor pumping is a separate service handled by haulers. We help with the plumbing around it and can tell you whether the line is clean once it has been pumped.

Leaks in a commercial building

A building with a high water bill and no visible leak needs a systematic check. We isolate the meter, test branches and listen on the lines, the same method described on our leak detection page. Commercial leak work often takes longer because lines run through ceilings and shared walls.

We tell you what we found and what the options are. In some cases the answer is a repair, and in others a landlord or another tenant is responsible, and that is a conversation for you and your property manager.

Commercial plumbing around Las Tunas Drive and Rosemead Boulevard

Las Tunas Drive is Temple City's main commercial corridor, and Rosemead Boulevard (State Route 19) runs along the city's west side. Our office is on Las Tunas Drive, so a call from either corridor is a short dispatch.

In older multi-tenant buildings, units often share a drain line. A backup in one unit can come from a blockage in the shared line, which is why we camera the line and talk to the property manager. The sewer mains belong to the Los Angeles County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District, and the laterals to the buildings are the owners'.

For backflow device testing and similar compliance work, ask your water utility what it requires for your property. We can tell you what the work involves, though some tests must be done by a certified tester the utility accepts.
