Why San Gabriel's plumbing calls are mostly about age

The city's housing element reports 13,564 housing units in San Gabriel. Of those, 52.3 percent are more than 60 years old, compared with 32.1 percent across the Southern California region. The largest group, 18.7 percent, was built in the 1940s, and another 17.1 percent dates from 1939 or earlier.

San Gabriel's water and your water heater

Golden State Water lists San Gabriel in its San Gabriel customer service area, which includes the South San Gabriel system. That system reported 2025 hardness from 189 to 310 mg/L, an average of about 240 mg/L, or 14 grains per gallon.

In water that hard, a tank water heater collects scale on the bottom and on the elements. A water heater that has been running for more than ten years may already be coated, and the next step is often a leak. Our water heater replacement page explains how a same-day swap works, and tankless water heaters need a yearly flush with water this hard.

Repiping an older home usually involves a permit under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Ask the building department in your city what your project needs.

Wet-season stress on old sewer laterals

Southern California's rainy months soak the ground around old clay laterals, and roots grow toward the moisture and the joints that leak it. A line that was fine in summer can back up after a series of storms. If your drains slow down after the first rains, ask for a camera check before the next one.

For a sewer backup, our sewer line repair service covers the lateral, and a blockage that has not damaged the pipe is handled by drain cleaning. The mains in the street belong to the public agency, and we show you on camera where your responsibility ends.
