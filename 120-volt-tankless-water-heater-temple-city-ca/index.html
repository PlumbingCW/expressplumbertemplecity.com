120 Volt Tankless Water Heater: A Temple City Homeowner Guide

A 120 volt tankless water heater plugs into an ordinary outlet and heats water as it passes through, but it can only run a small flow. This guide explains the limits with simple math and shows where one makes sense in a house.

By the Temple City Express Plumbing team · November 13, 2026

24/7 Emergency Line Open

What a 120 volt tankless unit is

Tankless water heaters heat water on demand. Large ones run on gas or on 240 volt circuits and are sized to supply a whole house. A 120 volt model is a small electric unit that connects to a standard household circuit and is meant for one location, such as a bathroom sink, a bar or a workshop.

They are attractive because no new circuit or gas line is needed. The catch is power. Heating water takes a lot of energy, and a standard circuit cannot supply much.

The math: why it will not run a shower

A 15 amp, 120 volt circuit can supply at most about 1,800 watts, and a continuous load is normally limited to around 1,440 watts. A shower at 2 gallons per minute heated by 50 degrees Fahrenheit needs about 50,000 BTU per hour, which is roughly 15 kilowatts. That is more than ten times what the outlet can deliver.

The same 1,440 watts will heat a trickle of water. That is enough for rinsing hands at a sink if the water is already lukewarm, and not enough for a shower, a dishwasher or a washing machine. Manufacturers describe these units as point-of-use heaters for this reason.

Safety guidance before you plug anything in: a circuit breaker and wire size are rated for a set load, and what to do if a plug-in unit trips the breaker repeatedly is stop using it and call an electrician or plumber.

Where a small unit makes sense

A 120 volt heater fits a place where hot water has to travel a long way and you only need a little. A powder room at the far end of a house is typical, as is a laundry sink or a workshop. Some people also use them as booster heaters ahead of one fixture.

A better answer for a whole house is a gas or 240 volt tankless unit, or a tank. We explain the choice on our tankless water heater page, and if you are planning a larger swap, water heater installation covers sizing and code items.

Installing a point-of-use heater

A small unit mounts under a sink or on a wall near the fixture. It connects to the cold supply and the hot outlet, with a shutoff valve on the inlet. It needs a grounded outlet on a circuit that is not overloaded, and it should be protected by a ground fault outlet where the code calls for one.

Do not share the circuit with other heavy loads. A tankless unit that trips a breaker is not defective, it is overloaded. If you are unsure whether an outlet is on a dedicated circuit, an electrician or plumber can check.

What to know in Temple City

Water hardness matters for any tankless unit, because scale builds inside narrow passages. Golden State Water's South San Gabriel system averaged about 240 mg/L in 2025, or 14 grains per gallon, and its South Arcadia system averaged 160 mg/L. A small heater at a sink in a house with hard water can clog in a few years if it is never flushed.

Temple City is mostly single-family houses, with 88.1 percent of units in that group, and a point-of-use heater is a common fix in a house where the bathroom is far from the water heater. Permit and electrical rules vary by job, so ask the Temple City building department about yours.

If you live on the San Gabriel or El Monte side of the area, the same considerations apply, as our San Gabriel and El Monte pages describe.

Power needed versus power available

The relationship is simple. The power needed in kilowatts equals flow in gallons per minute times 500 times the temperature rise in degrees Fahrenheit, divided by 3,412. The table below shows what that means for a few real situations.

FlowTemperature risePower neededFits a 120 volt outlet?

Even the smallest line of that table exceeds a 120 volt circuit. That is why these units are sold for tiny flows and short uses, and why the water they deliver is often only warm. Be skeptical of any claim that one can run a full shower.

Safety and installation notes for a point-of-use unit

Use a grounded outlet and follow the manual for the circuit. A small unit under a sink is in a damp place, so a ground fault protected outlet is the safe choice, and the code may call for one. Keep the unit away from stored items, leave room to service it and install a shutoff valve on the inlet.

A pressure relief device or a pressure and temperature relief valve may be required depending on the model and the code. Read the manual and ask the Temple City building department how permits apply. If any of this is unclear, have a plumber and an electrician check the setup before it is switched on.

Alternatives to a small electric unit

If the issue is waiting for hot water at a far fixture, a recirculating pump can bring hot water faster. If the issue is capacity, a larger tank or a whole-house tankless unit is the answer. If the issue is a single sink in an addition, a small unit under the sink can work.
