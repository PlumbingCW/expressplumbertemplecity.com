An emergency plumber in Temple City, at any hour

An emergency plumber is a licensed plumber who answers outside business hours for problems that cannot wait until morning, such as a burst pipe, a sewer backup or a water heater that is flooding a garage. Temple City Express Plumbing runs a 24/7 emergency line for Temple City, CA, and we answer it nights, weekends and holidays.

Most calls we take here start with a symptom, not a diagnosis. The water meter is still spinning with every tap off. A ceiling stain keeps growing. The kitchen sink gurgles when the washer drains. Tell us what you see and we will work out the likely cause on the phone, then send a plumber who can handle it on arrival.

Because Temple City's homes are mostly single-family houses from the mid-century building boom, the calls follow a pattern. Burst pipe repair and leak detection cover aging supply lines, drain cleaning handles the stoppages that come with old branch lines, and sewer line repair deals with roots and cracks in the lateral that runs to the street. A failed tank is a job for water heater repair or, when the tank is past saving, water heater replacement.

The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

What you do before we arrive limits the damage. These four steps work for most emergency plumbing calls in Temple City, and you can follow them while you wait on the phone with dispatch.

1Shut off the waterTurn the main valve clockwise. It is usually at the street meter box or where the supply line enters the house.
2Stop using drainsNo flushing, laundry or dishwasher when a drain is backing up. Every flush adds to the overflow.
3Cut power near waterIf water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off that circuit and stay out of standing water.
4Call the 24/7 lineCall (626) 898-6169, tell us what you see, and keep photos of the damage for your insurer.

If you smell gas, leave the house first and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Gas is the one problem where the phone call to us comes second.

Why Temple City plumbing fails the way it does

Temple City was laid out in 1923 by Walter P. Temple on part of the old Rancho Santa Anita and incorporated as a city in 1960. The housing that followed is what matters to a plumber. The city's own housing element, built on American Community Survey estimates, shows how old the stock really is.

30.6%of Temple City homes were built in the 1950s, the single largest decade in the city's housing stock.
57.7%of homes are more than 60 years old, against 32.1% across the Southern California region.
88.1%of housing units are single-family homes, so most plumbing problems sit inside a private lot.

A house built in the 1950s is now past the working life of its original supply lines and drain pipes. Depending on the builder and later remodels, that can mean galvanized steel that is closing up with scale, older copper with pinhole corrosion, or clay and cast-iron drain lines that roots have found. If your home is in that group, a recurring clog or a pressure drop is usually telling you something about the pipe, not the fixture.

Single-family lots also mean the sewer lateral and the water service line belong to the homeowner. A backup from the lateral is your repair, while a break in the public sewer main in the street is the district's. We sort out which side of that line you are on during the visit, and it saves a lot of finger-pointing.

Hard water changes by street here

Temple City is served by more than one water system. Golden State Water Company's South San Gabriel system reported 2025 hardness from 189 to 310 mg/L, an average of 240 mg/L or about 14 grains per gallon. Its South Arcadia system averaged 160 mg/L, about 9.4 grains. The Cal Am San Marino system, which also reaches parts of Temple City, reported an average of 162 mg/L, about 9.5 grains, in its 2023 report.

Those are published utility figures, not a reading from your tap. Hard water builds scale on heating elements and tank bottoms, so a water heater on the harder side of town can fail sooner. Our water softener and filtration page explains how to find out which system supplies your address.
