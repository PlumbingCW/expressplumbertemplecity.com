When a slab leak needs a same-day call

A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs beneath or inside a concrete foundation. Most are slow, and the first sign is often a bill that jumps or a meter that moves with everything off. A few become urgent fast, with water pushing up through flooring, a warm patch on the floor that is getting bigger, or the sound of water running when no tap is open.

If any of those describe your house, close the main valve and call. We take these calls at night and on weekends, and a same-day visit is available when we have an opening. Keep shoes on in the wet area, move anything valuable off the floor and switch off breakers for outlets near the water.

Signs of a slab leak

  • ✓A floor that feels warm in one spot, which points to a hot water line leaking beneath it.
  • ✓A hissing or trickling sound that continues with taps closed.
  • ✓Carpet or flooring that is damp, stained or lifting along baseboards.
  • ✓Low water pressure that has crept down over a few weeks.
  • ✓A water meter that moves when every fixture is off.

None of these proves a slab leak. A dripping tap, a running toilet or a leak in a wall can mimic it. That is why we confirm with listening equipment and a pressure test before anything is opened.

Cutting a slab or replacing pipe is usually permit work under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Ask the Temple City building department what applies to your job.

How we locate and confirm it

We begin by isolating the house from the street with the main closed and checking the meter. If it moves with the main closed, the leak is between the meter and the house. If it does not, we pressure-test the hot and cold lines separately, which tells us which one is leaking.

Then we narrow the location. Electronic listening gear picks up the sound of escaping water through the concrete, and a thermal camera shows where a hot line is heating the floor. The result is a spot on the floor, not a guess, so any opening is small and placed with care.

Repair choices, from spot repair to rerouting

There are three common ways to deal with a slab leak. We explain all three and price them on request, because the best choice depends on how many lines have failed and how accessible they are.

Slab leak repair options compared
OptionHow it worksBest suited to
Spot repair through the slabCut a small opening, repair or replace the damaged sectionOne accessible leak on an otherwise sound line
Reroute through walls or atticAbandon the slab line and run new pipe overheadRepeated leaks or a line that is hard to reach
Repipe the affected circuitReplace the full hot or cold runSeveral failures on the same aged pipe, see repiping

Rerouting avoids breaking the slab again, which is a common reason homeowners choose it when a line has leaked more than once.

Slab and raised-floor homes around Temple City

Houses in Temple City are not all built the same way. Some sit on concrete slabs and others on raised floors above a crawlspace, and the foundation type changes how we find and reach a leak. Raised-floor houses let us see and touch the pipes, and slab houses hide them.

The age of the pipe matters as much as the foundation. Temple City's housing element reports that 30.6 percent of homes were built in the 1950s, and a water line laid then has seen decades of the area's water. Golden State Water's South San Gabriel system reported hardness between 189 and 310 mg/L in 2025, and mineral-laden water is hard on thin copper walls.

The 1950s were the largest building decade citywide, so the pattern holds from the blocks around Live Oak Park to the streets off Rosemead Boulevard and Las Tunas Drive. When a house has had one slab leak, a pressure survey of the rest of the lines is worth the time.
