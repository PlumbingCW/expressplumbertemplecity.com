Plumbing Installation in Temple City, CA

What plumbing installation covers

We install fixtures and supply and drain lines in homes and small commercial spaces. That includes sinks, faucets, toilets, showers and tubs, laundry connections, dishwasher and disposal lines, and new water lines to an addition.

We also work on the planning stage. A bathroom moved to a new spot needs a drain slope, a vent and supply lines that all have to fit between the joists. The earlier we see the plan, the fewer surprises when the walls open.

Replacing a toilet is covered under toilet repair. Fixing a leaking tap is a repair, not an installation, and is covered under faucet and valve repair.

New fixtures, drains and vents follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which covers trap sizing, venting and fixture connections.

Getting the rough-in right

Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls close, and it decides what you can install afterward. Drains need slope, vents need to reach open air or an air admittance device where code allows, and supply lines need shutoff valves where you can reach them.

We install quarter-turn shutoffs at each fixture, label the main shutoff and leave you a quick note on where things are. In an older house, tying into existing lines is where surprises appear, and we check pipe material and condition before we connect anything new.

Gas lines and other jobs you may be wondering about

Gas lines for a range, dryer or water heater are a safety-critical type of plumbing work. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us. For a planned gas connection, we explain what the job needs and what testing is done afterward.

Sump and ejector pumps matter for houses with a basement or a below-grade bathroom, which are uncommon in Temple City. If your property has one, tell us the model and we will say whether we can service it.

Planned water heater work has its own page at water heater installation, where sizing and code items are covered.

Permits and planning in Temple City

Temple City's municipal code adopts the 2025 California Plumbing Code, so new work is built to that edition. Permit rules vary by project, and the Temple City building department can tell you whether yours needs one and what inspections follow.

Most Temple City properties are single-family lots, with 88.1 percent of housing units in that category, so many installation jobs are additions, remodels and accessory units on existing lots. Older houses may have supply and drain lines that cannot support new fixtures, and we explain what the existing lines can handle.

If an older house is being opened up, it is a good time to check for aging supply lines and consider repiping before the walls are closed again.
