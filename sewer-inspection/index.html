Sewer Camera Inspection in Temple City, CA

Why look inside the sewer line

The lateral that carries waste from your house to the street is hidden under the yard. Problems build for years before they show up as a backup. A camera inspection turns a guess into a picture, and it is the cheapest way to find out the condition of a line before making a decision that depends on it.

There are four common reasons to book one: buying a house, planning a remodel or an addition, noticing slow drains in several rooms, and having a line cleared more than once in a year.

How an inspection works

  1. 01

    Access

    We open a cleanout, or remove a toilet if there is none.

  2. 02

    Run the camera

    A waterproof camera on a flexible rod travels down the line with a light.

  3. 03

    Locate and record

    We watch live, note the depth and distance of anything unusual and record video.

  4. 04

    Review together

    We explain the footage and what it means for your decision.

Any repair that follows an inspection falls under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the video helps the plumber and the building department agree on what the job needs.

What the camera can and cannot show

A camera shows pipe material, joints, roots, cracks, offset sections, bellies where water stands and debris. A locating device can mark the line's path and depth at the surface, which is useful for planning work.

It cannot tell you how long a pipe will last, and it does not replace a code inspection. It shows condition today. If the footage shows a blockage or damage, the next step may be sewer line cleaning or sewer line repair, and we explain which fits.

When a camera inspection helps most in Temple City

In a city where 57.7 percent of homes are over 60 years old, the laterals under many yards are as old as the houses. Clay pipe, cast iron and fiber pipe from the 1950s can all be in service, and each shows different signs on camera.

A buyer evaluating an older Temple City house gets the most from an inspection, since a sewer lateral replacement is a major repair that can change the price of a purchase. A homeowner planning an addition, or a new patio over the line, should look before building anything on top of it.

Public sewer mains in Temple City are maintained by the Los Angeles County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District. Our camera covers your lateral up to the connection, and we tell you if we see a problem that looks like it belongs to the district.
