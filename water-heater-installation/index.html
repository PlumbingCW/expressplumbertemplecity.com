Water Heater Installation in Temple City, CA

Replacing a water heater before it fails lets you choose the unit, the fuel and the schedule, and a planned install usually costs less than a flooded garage. Call (626) 898-6169 to book a visit, and we will measure the space, check the gas, vent and electrical supply and explain the options.

Why plan a water heater install before the tank fails

A tank heater that is ten to fifteen years old is living on borrowed time. Replacing it on your own schedule means you pick the model, the work is done in one planned visit and the old unit never has the chance to flood the garage.

If your heater is already leaking, skip to our water heater replacement page, which covers emergency swaps. This page is for planned work, including a first-time install in a remodel, an upgrade in size or a change of fuel.

What we check before we install

  1. 01

    Space and access

    Clearances, floor condition, drain location and the path the new tank will take.

  2. 02

    Fuel and venting

    Gas line size, vent path and combustion air, or the electrical circuit and breaker for an electric unit.

  3. 03

    Water and pressure

    Static pressure, shutoff valves and whether the system is closed and needs an expansion tank.

  4. 04

    Code items

    Seismic bracing, a relief valve and discharge pipe, and permit questions for the city.

Installations follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and your city's permit process. Ask the Temple City building department whether your job needs a permit before work starts.

Choosing a type and size

Most single-family households in Temple City are served by a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Larger households with several bathrooms or a soaking tub may want 65 gallons, and some choose a tankless unit to avoid running out. The right capacity depends on how many people shower at once, not only on the house size.

Gas tank heaters are the least expensive to buy and are common in older homes with an existing gas line. Electric units are simpler to install and fit where gas is not available. Tankless water heaters deliver continuous hot water, and heat pump hybrids use less electricity but need space and airflow.

Expansion tanks, straps and relief valves

Water expands when it is heated. In a closed system, where a check valve or a pressure regulator stops water from flowing back to the street, that expansion has nowhere to go and pressure climbs. A small expansion tank absorbs it and protects the heater and the fixtures. Whether you need one depends on your system, and we check with a gauge.

California requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped under Health and Safety Code 19211. We install straps on every heater, and a properly routed relief valve discharge pipe is part of the job. Permit rules vary by city, so ask the Temple City building department whether your install needs one.

Temple City water and your new heater

A new heater is a long-term purchase, and local water affects how long it lasts. Golden State Water's South San Gabriel system reported 2025 hardness from 189 to 310 mg/L, with an average of about 240 mg/L, or 14 grains per gallon. Its South Arcadia system averaged 160 mg/L and the Cal Am San Marino system, which also reaches parts of Temple City, reported 162 mg/L in its 2023 report.

On the harder side, scale accumulates faster. A yearly flush of the tank and a look at the anode rod help the heater reach the end of its warranty. If you want to reduce scale throughout the house, read our water softener and filtration page before choosing equipment.
