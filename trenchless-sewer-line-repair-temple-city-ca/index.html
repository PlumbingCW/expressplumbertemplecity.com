Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Temple City: Your Options

Trenchless sewer repair fixes a damaged line without digging a long trench. It works well in some cases and not in others. This guide explains the two main methods, when each fits and what to ask before you choose.

By the Temple City Express Plumbing team · November 13, 2026

24/7 Emergency Line Open

What trenchless repair means

A traditional sewer repair opens a trench from the surface to the pipe, replaces the damaged section and refills. Trenchless methods repair or replace the pipe from inside, through small access points at each end. The yard, driveway or patio above stays mostly intact.

There are two main trenchless methods. One lines the existing pipe with a new resin liner. The other pulls a new pipe through the old one while breaking it apart. Neither is right for every line, and the camera inspection decides.

Pipe lining and pipe bursting

The two main trenchless sewer methods
MethodHow it worksBest suited to
Cured-in-place liningA resin-soaked liner is pulled in, inflated and hardened into a new pipe inside the old oneCracks, root entry, leaking joints in a pipe that is open and mostly round
Pipe burstingA cone pulls a new pipe through the old one, breaking it outwardCollapsed, badly offset or very old pipe that needs to be replaced

Lining reduces the diameter of the pipe a little, but the smooth liner usually flows as well as or better than the old pipe. Bursting replaces the pipe entirely and needs entry and exit pits.

A trenchless repair on a lateral still needs a permit, and the work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and local requirements.

When trenchless does not fit

A pipe that is fully collapsed, has a severe belly or has sharp offsets cannot always be lined. A section that holds standing water has a low spot that a liner will keep. Some lines have too many bends or connections to pull a liner or a burst head through.

In those cases a spot repair by excavation is the honest answer. It costs a trench, but it fixes the actual defect. We show you the camera footage and explain which method fits and why. If the problem is a blockage and not damage, our sewer line repair page explains the difference.

How the process goes

  1. 01

    Inspect

    A camera shows the condition and length of the pipe.

  2. 02

    Clean

    The line is cleaned, often by jetting, so the liner or pipe can pass.

  3. 03

    Line or burst

    The liner is installed and cured, or the new pipe is pulled through.

  4. 04

    Verify

    A final camera pass confirms the result and the connections are restored.

Many trenchless jobs are done in a day or two, though the time depends on length and conditions.

Trenchless repair on Temple City laterals

Temple City is part of the Los Angeles County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District, which maintains the public sewer mains. Your lateral, from the house to the main, is yours, and that is the pipe a trenchless repair addresses.

The city's housing element shows that 57.7 percent of homes are more than 60 years old and 88.1 percent are single-family houses, so most laterals are private runs across a yard. A trenchless repair saves landscaping, a driveway or a patio, which can matter more than the cost difference.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Temple City building department about yours. Neighbors in San Gabriel and Arcadia have their own city offices and rules, and our location pages explain what we see there.

What lining is made of and what to ask

A liner is a flexible tube soaked in resin. Once it is in place and cured with hot water, steam or light, the resin hardens into a rigid pipe inside the old one. The result is a continuous pipe without joints, which is why lining blocks root entry.

Ask what material is used, what its design life is and how connections to the house and the main are restored. Ask whether the liner is rated for the pipe's size and the flow. Ask to see the final camera footage. A contractor who does this work regularly can show you examples, and any warranty should be in writing.

The footage decides. If you want a second look, a sewer inspection from us gives you the video and our read of it.

What the yard and the schedule look like

A trenchless job still needs some digging. Expect one or two access pits, usually near the house and near the street or the property line, plus equipment on the driveway. Plan for a day or two without sewer service in some cases, and arrange a place to stay if the house will be without toilets for long.

After the work, the pits are filled and the surface restored. Landscaping near the pits may need replanting. Ask your contractor what they will repair and what is left to you, and get the answer in writing before work starts.

Questions to ask before you choose

Ask to see the camera footage. Ask why the recommended method fits the defect. Ask what is covered if the repair fails, what the warranty is and what happens at the connections to the house and the main. Ask what permits and inspections apply. A sewer inspection before any repair gives you the footage and a second opinion.
