Two water utilities and a gap in hardness

Rosemead is served by more than one water provider. Golden State Water lists Rosemead in its San Gabriel customer service area, and the San Gabriel County Water District, which is headquartered on Grand Avenue in Rosemead, serves a part of the area from groundwater wells in the Main San Gabriel and Raymond basins. The district's 2025 report lists total hardness from 17 to 200 mg/L with an average of 97.4 mg/L, about 5.7 grains per gallon.

Golden State Water's South San Gabriel system, by contrast, averaged about 240 mg/L in 2025, or 14 grains. A house on one side of a boundary can have water that is more than twice as hard as a house on the other, and the difference shows up in how fast scale builds on water heaters.

Check your water bill for the utility's name. If your water is on the harder side, the advice on our water heater repair page about flushing and anode rods applies to you.

Older buildings, older pipes

With 78.5 percent of the housing stock at least 40 years old, pipe condition is the issue we see most in Rosemead. Galvanized steel narrows with rust, copper develops pinholes, and drain lines of clay or cast iron are prone to roots and scale. A line that fails once tends to fail again nearby.

For a house with repeated leaks, leak detection finds the current ones, and if the pipe is worn throughout, repiping is the lasting fix. For a stubborn drain, a camera inspection under our sewer line repair service shows what is under the yard.

Renters, landlords and shared lines

In a city where renter-occupied units outnumber owner-occupied ones, plumbing emergencies often involve a tenant and a landlord. Tenants should report a leak or a backup to the landlord at once. Landlords and property managers can call us directly, and we describe what failed so the record is clear.
