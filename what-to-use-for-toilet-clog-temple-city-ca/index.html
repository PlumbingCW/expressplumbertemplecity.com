What to Use for a Toilet Clog: A Temple City Homeowner Guide

Most toilet clogs clear with a flange plunger, and a toilet auger handles the rest. This guide explains what to use, what to avoid, and when a clog means a bigger problem in the drain line.

By the Temple City Express Plumbing team · November 13, 2026

24/7 Emergency Line Open

Start with the right plunger

A toilet needs a flange plunger, which has a soft rubber flap that folds out from the cup. It seals in the toilet drain. A flat cup plunger made for sinks does not seal in a toilet, and it is the reason many people give up too early.

Fit the plunger so the cup covers the drain opening, push down slowly to expel air, then pull and push with steady strokes. Ten to twenty strokes are often enough. Keep water in the bowl to cover the cup. If the bowl is nearly full, do not flush, and take water out first.

Pour a cup of dish soap into the bowl, then a bucket of hot tap water from waist height. Let it sit for ten minutes before plunging again.

Under CALGreen standards, toilets in new California installations use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, so the plunger technique matters more than extra flushing.

What to avoid

  • ✓Chemical drain cleaners, which can sit in a blocked bowl, damage old fittings and burn skin if they splash.
  • ✓Boiling water, which can crack porcelain.
  • ✓Flushing repeatedly, which spills water onto the floor.
  • ✓Wipes, which are sold as flushable and do not break down like paper.
  • ✓Forcing a hard tool into the bowl, which can scratch or chip it.

If a drain cleaner has already been poured in, tell the plumber. It changes how we work.

Using a toilet auger

A toilet auger, also called a closet auger, has a curved tip and a handle with a crank. Feed the tip into the drain, crank slowly while pushing in, and feel for the clog. When it breaks free, flush to confirm. A guard on the end protects the porcelain.

If the auger reaches the clog and does not clear it, or if water rises in other fixtures when you flush, the problem is probably in the drain line and not the toilet. That is where our drain cleaning page comes in.

Signs the clog is bigger than the toilet

A toilet that clogs once is usually a toilet problem. A toilet that clogs again after a day, or that bubbles when the shower drains, usually points to the line. If the tub or a floor drain backs up when you flush, the main line is full and you should stop using water.

If a toilet rocks or leaks at the base, that is a seal problem, not a clog, and our toilet repair page covers it.

What to know in Temple City

Public sewer mains in Temple City are maintained by the Los Angeles County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District, and wipes and other items that do not break down cause blockages in them as well as in house lines. The district advises against flushing anything but toilet paper and human waste.

Houses from the 1950s make up 30.6 percent of Temple City's housing. Their drain lines are old and often have roots or scale, so a toilet that clogs repeatedly in an older house is worth a camera look. In nearby cities, our pages for San Gabriel and El Monte describe the same pattern.

If sewage is coming out of a manhole near your house, the district's 24-hour line is (800) 675-4357.

Why toilets clog in the first place

A toilet works by siphon. Water from the tank rushes into the bowl and pushes waste through a curved passage called the trap, and the siphon pulls the rest along. A clog happens when something fills the trap or the passage and stops the siphon. Too much paper, wipes, a child's toy or a weak flush are common causes.

A flush that has become weak can come from mineral scale blocking the small holes under the rim, or from a fill level that is too low. Clearing those passages or adjusting the water level can help.

Preventing the next clog

  • ✓Flush only toilet paper and human waste.
  • ✓Keep a trash can in the bathroom, and give wipes and hygiene products a place to go that is not the toilet.
  • ✓Use less paper at a time, and flush twice for a large load.
  • ✓Keep a flange plunger in the bathroom, where you can reach it fast.
  • ✓Fix a weak flush before it turns into a clog.

A toilet that clogs more than a few times a year deserves a closer look. The fix may be a worn part, a scale problem or a drain line that needs cleaning, and a plumber can tell which.

What not to flush, and why it matters for the whole street

A toilet clog in your house is a small problem. Wipes, paper towels and similar items that reach the street can bunch up in the public sewer and cause backups for several homes. Grease and food scraps from the kitchen do the same when they cool in the pipe.

The safe list is short. Flush toilet paper and human waste only. Put everything else in the trash, including wipes labeled flushable. If you have a clog that keeps returning, tell the plumber what is regularly going down the toilet, since it changes the diagnosis.

When to call a plumber

Call if the plunger and auger do nothing, if the clog returns, if other fixtures back up or if something solid went down the toilet. If water is rising toward the rim, stop and close the valve behind the bowl. Our line at (626) 898-6169 is open 24/7.
